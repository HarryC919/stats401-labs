Lab 5: Interactive Network Visualization with D3

Urban Transit Network

A synthetic medium-sized city with 50 stations across five districts (Central, North, South, East, West), connected by 50 direct Metro, Express, and Shuttle routes. The same network is encoded twice: as an interactive force-directed node-link diagram and as an adjacency matrix.

Dataset Description

Stations and Routes

Each of the 50 stations carries three attributes: district (which of the five districts it sits in), daily_passengers (approximate average daily volume), and station_type (Local, Transfer, or Terminal). Each of the 50 routes is a direct, undirected connection between two stations with two attributes: travel_time_min and route_type (Metro, Express, or Shuttle).

Design Description

How the Node-Link View Is Encoded

District colors group stations; shapes distinguish Local, Transfer, and Terminal roles. Area is proportional to passengers, showing usage. Wider links indicate longer journeys; link colors distinguish services. Dragging separates overlapping marks, highlighting reveals neighbors, and tooltips identify stations.

Design Description

How the Matrix Is Encoded

Rows and columns represent stations; filled cells indicate connections. Cell color shows service type, opacity shows travel time, and label color shows district. Ordering by district, then station type, groups connections for comparison. Passenger volume remains in the node-link view to avoid cluttering cells.

Network Questions and Findings

Six Questions, Read from the Visualizations

1. Which stations appear central in the network?

Node-link, partially. Highlighted neighbors reveal degree centrality: Stations 4 and 7 each have three neighbors, sharing the maximum. Other centrality measures are not encoded.

2. Which districts are strongly connected to one another?

Matrix, well. District blocks reveal 11 East–South connections, versus nine each for Central–North and East–West; North–West has only one.

3. Where are transfer or terminal stations located in the topology?

Node-link, partially. Triangles and squares occur in all five district colors. Hovering reveals their immediate neighbors, but broader structural roles require tracing paths.

4. Which stations have high passenger volume?

Node-link, partially. Large areas indicate high usage; tooltips identify Stations 46–50 as the busiest (9,158–9,850 passengers/day). Similar sizes make exact ranking difficult.

5. Where are the longest direct travel-time connections?

Matrix, well. Opaque cells and tooltips identify Express connections 7–31 (16 minutes), 18–34 (15), and 29–50 (14) as the longest.

6. Are particular route types concentrated in particular parts of the network?

Matrix, well. Cell colors mix across major district pairs, but Central's two internal connections are both Shuttle; North–West's only connection is Metro.

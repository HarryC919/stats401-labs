Lab 4: Cleaning Web Data for Visualization

Tweet Sentiment over Time

This visualization shows how estimated tweet sentiment evolved during the first half of 2026, combining daily averages with a weekly trend line.

Analysis

The dataset contains 7,041 English-language tweets posted between January 1 and June 5, 2026. During cleaning, rows with missing text or invalid dates were removed, only records labeled as English Tweets were retained, embedded header rows and duplicate tweet IDs were dropped, numeric engagement fields were coerced, and HTML entities, tags, and irregular whitespace were normalized. For sentiment analysis, mentions and URLs were replaced with generic tokens while punctuation, emoji, capitalization, and negation were preserved. The Twitter RoBERTa model estimated negative, neutral, and positive probabilities for each tweet; sentiment score was calculated as P(positive) − P(negative), so higher values indicate more positive language. Each blue point encodes the mean score for one day, while the red line summarizes weekly means and the dashed line marks neutral sentiment. Sentiment remains positive throughout the period, but the weekly trend generally declines: averages are strongest in January, flatten near 0.40 in March and April, and reach their lowest levels in late May and early June. Daily values fluctuate around this smoother downward pattern.

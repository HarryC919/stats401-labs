Design Description

Encoding and Mental Map

Node color encodes sector, node stroke encodes region (solid dark for Asia, dashed dark for Europe, white for North America), and node size encodes the current day’s transaction volume. Link color encodes transaction type and link width encodes amount, so two company attributes and two link properties are visible at once.

To preserve the mental map, node positions are computed once with d3.forceSimulation() on the aggregated 60-day network and then fixed; frames only join and exit links and resize nodes, so no company ever jumps to a new location. New relationships fade in and ended ones fade out over 400 ms, and the animation is fully controllable with play, pause, reset, and a 60-day slider.

Analysis

Temporal Network Questions

1. How does overall connectivity change across the 60 days? The network becomes denser and more valuable over time. The first ten days average about 5.5 active links and roughly $77k per day; the last ten days average about 7.4 links and roughly $149k per day — daily transaction value approximately doubles.

2. Which companies become more central or commercially active over time? Fusion Electronics is the most active company overall (about $2.0M in total volume). Ion Systems grows the most dramatically, from about $321k in the first half to about $1.17M in the second, and Cedar Retail roughly triples ($316k → $971k). BlueRiver Logistics is the only large company whose volume slightly declines.

3. Are there periods of relatively separate clusters? On individual days the network is fragmented: only 3–10 of the 66 possible links are active per day, so a typical day splits into about 3–9 small components with several isolated companies. Over any 10-day window, however, all 12 companies connect into a single component, so the separation is day-level sparsity rather than durable clusters.

4. Which relationships appear, disappear, or become stronger? BlueRiver–Delta, Cedar–Fusion, and Harbor–Keystone trade only in the first half and then disappear. New second-half ties include Cedar–Keystone ($282k), Fusion–Ion ($301k), and Cedar–Granite ($254k). Ion–Juniper strengthens ($127k → $202k), while Granite–Harbor weakens sharply ($175k → $67k).

5. Does the network become more or less cross-regional over time? More cross-regional. The share of links between companies in different regions holds around 62–65% during days 1–40, then rises to about 72–73% over days 41–60.

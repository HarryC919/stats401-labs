Design Description

Why These Channels and Tilings

I chose rectangle area to encode GDP because it shows each country’s contribution to the total within the continent and area hierarchy. Larger economies occupy more space, making broad differences easy to identify, although similar values are harder to compare precisely by area. Color encodes GDP status because increase, unchanged, and decrease are distinct categories. This separates the direction of change from the size of an economy, while the legend explains the category mapping.

Squarified tiling favors compact rectangles with aspect ratios close to one, making country areas easier to compare and leaving more usable space for labels. Slice-dice tiling alternates horizontal and vertical divisions at successive hierarchy levels, emphasizing the recursive grouping structure. However, unequal GDP values can produce long, thin rectangles that make smaller countries and their labels difficult to read. Both views use the same GDP values and status colors, so their differences show how layout affects readability without changing the underlying data.

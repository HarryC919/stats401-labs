Lab 8: Web Text Data and Visualization

Bulletin Semantic Explorer

The 2021–22 DKU Undergraduate Bulletin, read as both a formal document and a semantic space. 1,460 passages were embedded, clustered into eight topics, and projected with UMAP; the semantic map and the topic × section matrix below are coordinated so the bulletin’s official structure can be compared with what its text actually talks about.

Findings

Semantic Findings

1. What are the major semantic topics? Eight KMeans topics cover the corpus. The three largest are course-centric: STEM Courses and Major Requirements (295 passages, 20.2%), Politics, Economics, and Public Policy (271, 18.6%), and History, Culture, and the Arts (243, 16.6%). Language Learning, Writing, and Placement holds 193 passages (13.2%), University Mission, Services, and Study Opportunities 181 (12.4%), and Academic Progress, Grading, and Enrollment Status 147 (10.1%). The smallest topics are Academic Calendar and Class Scheduling (66, 4.5%) and Elective Choices and Course Availability (64, 4.4%). Together, course-list material makes up roughly 38% of the bulletin.

2. Which topics appear across multiple formal sections? University Mission, Services, and Study Opportunities appears in 62 of the 84 sections (74%) — advising, career, and study-away boilerplate recurs in nearly every chapter. Academic Progress and Grading appears in 32 sections (38%) and Language and Writing in 18 (21%). At the other extreme, STEM Courses is concentrated in only 9 sections (11%) despite being the largest topic, and Elective Choices occurs in just 3 sections — it is almost entirely the repeated “choose your electives” paragraph inside major pages (e.g., p00417_01, p00422_01).

3. Which formal sections are semantically most diverse? Measuring the normalized Shannon entropy of each section’s topic distribution, the two largest sections are also the most diverse: Majors (listed in alphabetical order) mixes 6 topics across 317 passages (entropy 0.71 of a maximum 1.0), and Course Descriptions mixes 6 topics across 674 passages (0.68). The small “Publication and institutional notices” section (9 passages) is also mixed (0.69), though its size makes that fragile. Most other sections sit below 0.52, and single-purpose sections such as the Mission Statement or the calendar are essentially one-topic. Diversity here tracks genuine content mixing, not just section size: both leaders draw topics 0–4 in large proportions.

4. Which passages from different sections are semantically similar? The neighbor panel surfaces many near-duplicates between the parallel “Academic Warning, Probation, and Suspension” sections written for the Classes of 2022–2024 (pp. 57–58) and the Class of 2025+ (pp. 59–60): for example p00240_01 ↔ p00251_01 at similarity 1.000 — the same policy restated per cohort. More revealing cross-chapter links include p00221_01 (Grading and Grade Requirements, p. 53) ↔ p00334_01 (Office of Global Education and Study Away, p. 75) at 0.961, both about how external grades and credits post to the DKU record; p00151_01 (Transfer of Work Taken Elsewhere, p. 39) ↔ p00333_01 (Study Away, p. 75) at 0.914, restating the same 40-credit external-transfer cap; and p00691_01 (Course Descriptions, p. 217) ↔ p00413_01 (Majors, p. 95) at 0.924, the “not every course is offered every term” disclaimer repeated across dozens of major pages.

5. Does credit live in one semantic region? Searching the passage text: “credit” matches 783 passages (53.6% of the corpus) across 31 sections and 7 of the 8 topics — clearly spread across several semantic regions, dominated by the course-list topics (STEM 190, History/Arts 181, Politics/Econ 166, Language 154) where every course description cites its credit value, plus 48 passages in Progress & Grading. By contrast “graduation” (33 passages, 18 sections) concentrates in Progress & Grading (18) and Mission & Services (13); “registration” matches only 9 passages in 7 sections (6 of them Progress & Grading); and “academic integrity” appears in just 3 passages across 2 sections — a rare, formally localized concept. The map makes the contrast visible: generic credit talk saturates the course regions, while integrity/registration form small, policy-bound pockets.

Design Description

Method and Encodings

This page connects the bulletin’s formal structure with its semantic structure. The 400-page 2021–22 bulletin was split into 1,460 paragraph-scale passages, each embedded with sentence-transformers all-MiniLM-L6-v2 (384 dimensions, L2-normalized, 256-token limit). UMAP (n_neighbors 15, min_dist 0.15, cosine metric, random_state 401) projects embeddings to two dimensions — proximity suggests semantic similarity, but the axes themselves carry no meaning. KMeans (k = 8, random_state 401) clusters the original normalized embeddings, not the 2D coordinates. Before labeling, I inspected centroid-near passages, peripheral passages, and characteristic TF-IDF terms for every cluster, producing labels such as “STEM Courses and Major Requirements.”

The semantic map encodes position (UMAP), color (topic), and circle area (word count, square-root scale). The matrix cross-tabulates the 84 formal sections with the eight topics; cell color encodes passage counts computed on the full corpus, switchable to within-section proportion. One shared state coordinates the views: section and topic filters intersect, text search narrows within that eligible set, clicking a matrix cell applies its section and topic to the map, and selecting a point shows its details and five precomputed nearest neighbors (cosine similarity on the original embeddings) while outlining its matrix cell. Neighbors hidden by filters are marked and can be revealed explicitly. Zoom and pan (drag, double-click, Ctrl/⌘+scroll) stay local to the map. The clearest finding: the bulletin’s largest formal sections are also its most semantically diverse, while identical boilerplate recurs across many majors and cohort-specific policy sections.

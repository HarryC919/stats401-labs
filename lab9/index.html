Lab 9: Geospatial Visualization

2025 GDP: Choropleth vs. Cartogram

The same IMF 2025 nominal-GDP estimates for the 50 largest economies, drawn twice: a choropleth where color encodes GDP over true geography, and a Dorling cartogram where area encodes GDP and shapes are deliberately distorted. Hovering or clicking a country in either map highlights it in both.

Dataset Description

2025 Nominal GDP — Top 50 Economies

The statistical data come from the IMF World Economic Outlook, October 2025, nominal-GDP series, current prices. The IMF notes that WEO observations may include IMF staff estimates where complete official data are unavailable and can later be revised. The file data/lab9_gdp_2025_top50.csv holds one row per economy (50 rows): the 50 largest economies by 2025 nominal GDP.

Variable Meaning
iso3 ISO-3 geographic identifier — the join key against the GeoJSON
country Country/economy name (shown in tooltips)
gdp_2025_billion_usd 2025 nominal GDP, current prices, billions of U.S. dollars
rank GDP rank among the included economies (1–50)

The geographic data are data/ne_50m_admin_0_countries.geojson, an export of Natural Earth 1:50m Admin 0 Countries (public domain). The two files are joined on the ISO-3 code on both sides. A few Natural Earth features carry "-99" in ISO_A3 (France and Norway among the top 50); the join repairs them via the always-populated ADM0_A3 field. Countries outside the top 50 have no GDP value — they are rendered as neutral "no data", never as zero-GDP economies.

View 1

2025 GDP Choropleth

Position and shape = real geography; color = 2025 nominal GDP on a log-transformed Blues ramp (rationale in the comparison below). Hover for tooltip + highlight, click to pin a selection, drag to pan, scroll to zoom.

This map presents statistical data only. Some regions shown are disputed; it does not represent the political position of any individual or group.

View 2

2025 GDP Cartogram

AREA — not color, not geographic size — represents 2025 nominal GDP. Shapes are intentionally distorted: this is a Dorling cartogram (each top-50 economy is a circle whose area is proportional to GDP; implemented in src/lab9.ts). Countries outside the top 50 are omitted because they have no GDP value. Colors reuse the choropleth scale. Hover/click for tooltip + linked highlight.

Design and Comparison

Choropleth vs. Cartogram

These two views encode the same 2025 nominal-GDP estimates with different visual variables, so each answers a different question.

In the choropleth, the darkest countries — the United States and China — dominate, yet geographic position and shape keep huge, mid-ranked countries visually important: Russia, Canada and Australia catch the eye despite ranking 9th–15th. In the cartogram, area encodes GDP, so the US and China balloon while Russia, Canada and Australia shrink to modest circles; India visibly overtakes the UK and France, and geographically tiny Singapore, Hong Kong and Taiwan become legible for the first time. Switching from color to area therefore reframes global economic size around productive economies rather than land area.

The choropleth preserves exact position, shape, adjacency and distance, so it best supports where-questions: locating a country, reading regional patterns, comparing neighbors. The cartogram distorts shape, position and adjacency into circles, sacrificing geography to make ranking and ratio comparisons immediate.

For color, GDP runs from 300 to 30,616 billion USD — heavily right-skewed — so a linear Blues ramp would leave every mid-ranked economy the same pale blue. I chose a log-transformed sequential scale (d3.scaleSequentialLog): equal color steps for equal ratios keeps differences like Poland, Switzerland and Taiwan visible, with a strictly positive domain over joined values only.

Neither map is better universally: use the choropleth for geographic context, the cartogram for magnitude.
